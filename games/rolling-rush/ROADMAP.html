Rolling Rush · Product Plan · v15 and beyond

What to build next, and what actually makes people buy coins

Eight new worlds, three ball sizes, nine power balls, and an economy designed around the two moments players reach for their wallet.

Nobody buys coins for something they can wait for.

Right now every coin in Rolling Rush buys a cosmetic that patience also buys. That's a shop, not an economy. Purchases happen at two moments: loss — you just died at a personal record — and desire — you want a specific thing and you're short. Everything below is built to create those two moments honestly.

The five levers, ranked by revenue

Ordered by what actually moves money in endless runners, not by what's fun to build.

Continue the run

Highest impact

You fall at 847 m — a new record by 60 m. Instead of the game-over screen, you get three seconds and one question. This is the single biggest revenue driver in the genre, and Rolling Rush doesn't have it.

Continue from 847 m? — 50 coins · price doubles each use in a run

The escalating price matters: it keeps a great run alive without letting anyone buy an infinite score, which would wreck the leaderboard.

Permanent upgrades

Infinite coin sink

Skins cap out — once you own 33 balls, coins are worthless. Upgrades never cap. This is what keeps coins valuable a hundred hours in.

Timewarp — jump to any world

Your idea, and a good one

Reaching Pyramids means surviving 2,400 m. Most players never see half the game they're being sold. Timewarp turns that frustration into a purchase: unlock any world and start runs there.

Unlock a world — 300 coins · Timewarp Pass, every world — 2,000 coins

It also quietly solves a retention problem: new worlds you've already built stop being invisible. Pair it with a world-select carousel on the start screen so players see the locked ones every single session.

Mystery crate

Collection pressure

Your reference game leans on this hard. A crate costs coins and always returns a skin you don't already own — no duplicates, no bad luck, no gambling mechanics. Cheaper per skin than buying directly, so it's a genuine deal, and it makes the collection screen the thing players chase.

Mystery Crate — 150 coins · always a skin you don't own

Daily streak & rotating deal

Retention, not revenue

A seven-day streak that pays 10/15/25/40/60/100/250 coins, and one featured skin at 40% off each day. Neither makes much money directly — they make people open the game, which is what makes everything else work.

Coin packs

Two changes to what you already have, both worth more than the packs themselves.

Starter Pack — $2.99, once per account

1,500 coins plus an exclusive skin nobody can earn. First-time offers convert several times better than any recurring pack because the buyer isn't judging value, they're judging entry. Show it after a player's third run, never before.

Coin Doubler — $4.99, permanent

Every coin earned for the rest of time counts twice. This reliably outsells consumable packs in this genre: it rewards the players who already love the game, and it makes every future session feel better rather than draining a balance.

Build order

v15

Continue & upgrades Ship first

The revive prompt and the five-track upgrade screen. No new art required, and it's the only phase that changes revenue on its own. Everything after this is content that the economy can now sell.

v16

Timewarp & world select

The world carousel, per-world unlocks, and the Pass. Makes the eight worlds you already built visible, which is why it comes before building eight more.

v17

Four new worlds — Volcano, Neon City, Orbit Station, Deep Reef

The four with the strongest mechanic hooks and the best clip potential. Low gravity and lava spurts film well.

v18

Ball sizes, power balls, mystery crate

Sizes and powers land together so the crate has a deep enough pool to feel worth opening.

v19

Remaining worlds, streaks, daily deal

Candy Run, Graveyard, Red Canyon, Cloud Kingdom, plus the retention layer once there's enough content to come back to.

Two constraints worth designing around now, not later.

Playables bans third-party payments. Everything monetary here has to compile out of the YouTube build — the same switch that already hides your coin packs. Keep coins earnable there and the game stays fully playable without a store.

Keep the crate honest. A crate that always gives a skin you don't own is a discount, not a loot box. Paid randomised rewards draw real regulatory attention in the EU and UK, get scrutinised on the app stores, and this game skews young. The no-duplicates rule keeps you clear of all of it and players prefer it anyway.
